About Flag Ratios: Relational Health & FAQ

Relationship science highlights behavioral cues that predict partnership longevity. Drawing from John Gottman's Sound Relationship House research and boundary theory (Cloud & Townsend), flag ratios contrast healthy collaborative engagement with defensive, retaliatory habits across dating, friendships, and work.

Self-Reflection Boundary: This quiz is an educational tool for exploring interpersonal communication tendencies. It is not a clinical assessment or relationship therapy substitute.

Four Key Relationship Dimensions

  • Communication: Direct, calm problem-solving versus passive-aggressive withdrawal or silent treatment.
  • Boundaries: Respecting privacy and autonomy versus intrusive snooping and enmeshment.
  • Emotional Maturity: Self-soothing and conflict de-escalation versus reactive blame and vindictiveness.
  • Loyalty: Steadfast reliability and honesty versus opportunistic betrayal or scorekeeping.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the psychological meaning of red and green flags?

A: Red flags indicate defensive mechanisms like avoidance or manipulation; green flags demonstrate emotional regulation, empathy, and clear mutual boundaries.

Q: Can someone change their relationship flag patterns?

A: Yes! Red flag reactions are often learned survival habits. Cultivating self-awareness, active listening, and healthy boundaries steadily fosters green flag responses.

Q: Is this test a clinical compatibility diagnosis?

A: No. It is an educational self-reflection prompt designed to explore relational habits. It does not replace couples therapy or psychiatric consultation.

Q: Are my quiz answers or results saved?

A: Never. All calculations run strictly inside your browser. No answers, scores, or flag ratios are ever transmitted to or stored on external servers.
